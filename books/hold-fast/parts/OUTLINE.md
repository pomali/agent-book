# Seeds

Parts not yet written out. Grow each for the reader in front of you, inside
canon and in the book's voice (see `BOOK.md`; parts 01, 02 and 06 are the
model for tone, length and structure). Every grown part ends with one
*Try it* and two or three *Checks*. Label it *canon-derived*.

---

## 03-heads-drives: Heads and drives
- needs: 02 · canon: D1–D6 · briefs: V5 · misconceptions: X6
- **must include**: head shape decides how the head sits (flush or proud);
  the drive is a separate choice; PH against PZ and how to tell them apart by
  eye; why Torx and hex took over; bit size matters as much as bit type;
  stripped heads are almost always a bit problem.
- **must not**: rank drives as simply good and bad; claim Phillips was
  designed to cam out (canon says only that it tends to).
- **shape**: open with the reader tipping out their own bit set or screw jar
  and sorting it. End with the habit: press the bit in, feel that it does not
  rock, then turn.

## 04-wood: Screws in wood
- needs: 01, 02 · canon: C6, T11, W1–W8, R6 · briefs: V6, W3 · misconceptions: X7, X8, X10
- **must include**: the two holes and what each is for; jacking; pilot sizes
  with the 4 mm example; splitting near ends; end grain; the two-thirds rule;
  why drywall screws snap; countersinking.
- **must not**: give pilot sizes beyond what canon supports; for other
  diameters teach the method (hold the drill bit in front of the screw: the
  bit should hide the core and leave the threads showing).
- **shape**: one real joint, start to finish: a batten onto a post. Each
  mistake is made once and its result described. End with the bit-in-front-of-
  the-screw test as the thing to remember.

## 05-metal: Bolts, nuts and washers
- needs: 01, 02 · canon: T12, M1–M9 · briefs: V7 · misconceptions: X9, X10
- **must include**: reading the head marking; what 8.8 means, worked from the
  numbers; matching nuts; what a washer is and is not for; engagement in
  tapped holes; tap drill = diameter − pitch; clearance holes; coarse or fine.
- **must not**: discuss structural or pressure-rated bolting standards.
- **shape**: begin with the marking on a real bolt head, as a code to be
  cracked. End with the reader able to order a bolt, nut and washer by full
  name.

## 07-loosening: Why things come loose
- needs: 06 · canon: C5, T10, L1–L7 · briefs: V8, A3 · misconceptions: X5, X13
- **must include**: self-locking holds only under steady load; sideways slip
  as the mechanism; settling as the quiet second cause; preload as first
  defence; bolt length; what works and what does not, with the spring washer
  handled fairly; nylon-insert nuts keep the nut, not the preload.
- **must not**: mock the spring washer's users; recommend high-strength
  threadlocker without saying it needs heat to undo.
- **shape**: a mystery. Something the reader owns keeps coming loose. Work out
  which of the two causes it is before proposing a cure.

## 08-walls: Fixing into walls
- needs: 01 · canon: A1–A6 · briefs: V9 · misconceptions: X12
- **must include**: find out what the wall is (knock, drill dust colour, a
  test hole where it will not show); how an expansion plug works; hole
  diameter, depth, cleaning; hollow walls; pull-out against hanging load;
  the rating on the box and what it assumes.
- **must not**: state any load a fixing can carry. Always the packaging.
  Ceiling and overhead fixings get the safety line.
- **shape**: hanging one shelf. The book's most-used part; keep it
  practical and short. Check for hidden pipes and cables is one sentence, and
  is not optional.

## 09-not-screws: When not to use a screw
- needs: 01 · canon: N1–N5, W7 · briefs: V10 · misconceptions: X8
- **must include**: nails for shear and speed; rivets for thin sheet and
  permanence; glue along the grain; joinery carrying load by shape; choosing
  by whether the joint must ever come apart again.
- **must not**: turn into a catalogue. Four alternatives, one reason each.
- **shape**: the same small box built four ways, each failing or lasting
  differently.

## 10-materials: Rust and other chemistry
- needs: 05 · canon: R1–R6, M3 · briefs: V11 · misconceptions: X9
- **must include**: indoor zinc against outdoor galvanising; A2 against A4;
  galvanic pairs with the stainless-in-aluminium example; galling and how to
  avoid it; stainless is weaker than 8.8; brass screws and the steel
  pathfinder.
- **must not**: give a full galvanic series; keep to the pairs a reader will
  meet.
- **shape**: three outdoor objects a few years on: a gate, a boat fitting, a
  bicycle. What happened to each and why.

## 11-choosing: Choosing, a walk-through
- needs: all · canon: any · briefs: W4, V12
- **must include**: a sequence of questions. What are the two materials? Will
  it come apart again? Which way does the load pull? Indoors or out? What can
  I reach and drill? Each answer narrows the choice and names the part where
  the reasoning lives.
- **must not**: introduce new facts. This part only connects.
- **shape**: run the questions on something the reader actually wants to
  build. If they have nothing, use: a workbench vice bolted through a wooden
  bench top. Finish by producing their own one-page cheat card (brief V12).
