# Canon tests: Hold Fast

Answer from canon only. Check yourself before the first session.

- Q: What is a bolt's main job in a joint?
  A: To create clamp load; friction between the parts then carries sideways load. (C1, C3)
- Q: Coarse pitch of M6? Of M10?
  A: 1.0 mm; 1.5 mm. (T5)
- Q: What do the numbers on an 8.8 bolt mean?
  A: 800 MPa nominal tensile strength; yield 640 MPa. (M1)
- Q: Roughly how much of the tightening torque stretches the bolt?
  A: About 10 %. The rest is friction under the head and in the threads. (Q4)
- Q: Approximate dry torque for an M8 class 8.8 bolt, and what overrides it?
  A: About 25 N·m; a manufacturer's figure always wins. (Q7)
- Q: I oiled the threads and used the dry torque. Fine?
  A: No. Lower friction means much more preload for the same torque; the bolt may be overloaded. (Q3, Q5, X4)
- Q: Pilot hole for a 4 mm wood screw in hardwood?
  A: About 2.5 mm, roughly the root diameter. (W3)
- Q: Pilot hole for a 6 mm wood screw in oak?
  A: Canon has no figure. Teach the method (the bit should hide the core and leave the threads showing) or send them to the screw maker's data. Do not supply a number.
- Q: Does a spring washer stop a bolt loosening under vibration?
  A: Tests show little effect. Enough preload, wedge-locking washers, threadlocker or prevailing-torque nuts do. (L5, L6, X5)
- Q: Are Phillips and Pozidriv interchangeable?
  A: No. (D2, X6)
- Q: Is stainless stronger than an 8.8 steel bolt?
  A: No. A2/A4-70 is 700 MPa tensile, about 450 MPa yield, and can gall. (M3, R3, R4)
- Q: How much weight will an 8 mm plug hold in my ceiling?
  A: The book gives no loads. Use the rating on the packaging for that base material; overhead fixings get the safety line. (A5, A6, BOOK rule 3)
- Q: Will an M8 bolt fit a 5/16-18 nut?
  A: It will start and then bind and damage both. (T8, X11)
- Q: Which pedal on a bicycle has a left-hand thread?
  A: The left one. (T9)
