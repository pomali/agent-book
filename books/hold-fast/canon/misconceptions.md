# Canon: misconceptions

When the reader says or implies one of these, deal with it then. Each entry:
what people believe, why it is reasonable, what is true, and the quickest way
to show it.

**X1. "The bolt holds the parts like a pin through them."**
Reasonable: you can see the bolt go through. True: the bolt clamps; friction
between the parts carries the sideways load (C1–C4). Show: squeeze two books
together hard and try to slide one; then loosen your grip.

**X2. "Tighter is always better."**
Reasonable: loose is clearly bad. True: past yield the bolt stretches
permanently or the thread strips, and clamp load is lost; in wood the fibres
crush and the screw spins (Q6, W8). Show: the feel of a screw "going soft".

**X3. "A torque wrench measures how tight the bolt is."**
True: it measures the effort of turning, about 90 % of which is friction
(Q4, Q5). Same torque, different lubrication, very different preload.

**X4. "Oil the threads so it tightens properly, then use the book torque."**
True: lubrication lowers K, so a dry-torque value over-tightens (Q3, Q5). Use
the torque that matches the stated thread condition.

**X5. "A spring washer stops it coming loose."**
Reasonable: they are sold for that and feel springy. True: once flattened it
is a poor flat washer; tests show little effect against vibration (L5). What
works is in L2 and L6.

**X6. "Phillips and Pozidriv are the same cross."**
True: different geometry; mixing them is the classic way to ruin a head (D2).
Show: find the four fine lines on a PZ screw.

**X7. "Pilot hole and clearance hole are the same thing."**
True: one lets the screw pass, the other lets the thread bite (W1, W2).

**X8. "A screw is stronger than a nail."**
True: stronger against pulling out, often weaker and more brittle sideways
(N1, N2, W7). Framing is nailed for a reason.

**X9. "Stainless is the strongest."**
True: it resists rust; common stainless grades are weaker than 8.8 steel and
can gall (R3, R4).

**X10. "A longer screw always holds better."**
True in wood up to a point, if it stays in sound material (W6). In a tapped
metal hole, engagement beyond about 1–2 diameters adds nothing: the bolt
breaks first (M6).

**X11. "If it starts by hand, the thread matches."**
True: near-matches such as M8 and 5/16-18 start and then destroy each other
(T8). Two or three free turns by fingers, then it should keep going freely.

**X12. "Any plug works in any wall."**
True: an expansion plug in plasterboard or hollow block has nothing to expand
against (A1, A4).

**X13. "Vibration shakes the nut round."**
Closer: sideways slipping of the joint briefly removes thread friction and the
bolt's own tension unwinds it (L1). That is why enough preload is the cure.

**X14. "Righty-tighty always."**
Nearly. Left-hand threads exist exactly where it matters (T9).
