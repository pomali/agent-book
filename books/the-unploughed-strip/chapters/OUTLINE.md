## 03: Fire on the Hill (St John, June)
- canon: T3, T4, D2, P6, U3, U8, F4, V3
- **must include**: haymaking with scythes; the first rain, too late for the
  oats; St John's fires on the hill and the young jumping them; the Share
  mown for the first time in the year, the day after St John, by Matej alone,
  with a scythe and never a sickle; Jakub and Trudka speak, and she tells him
  what her father's settlers are promised: twelve years without rent.
- **must not**: let Jakub decide anything yet; show any unseen thing to two
  people.
- **shape**: begins in the hay, ends on the hill at night. Last image: from
  the fire Katka can see the whole valley, and the Share is the only dark
  line in the mown fields.

## 04: The Noon Wife (harvest, July)
- canon: U10, U2, U3, T4, D2, F3, F5, H1
- **must include**: reaping rye on the Upper with sickles, women reaping and
  men binding; the heat; everyone lies up at noon except Katka, who goes
  back for a forgotten sickle; the Noon Wife; her questions are ordinary and
  exact (how flax is sown, pulled, retted, broken, combed, spun) and Katka
  must answer truly until the bell; one question is not about flax: "Who
  counts for your house?" and Katka hears herself say "I do."; the last
  handful of rye left standing and tied.
- **must not**: have the Noon Wife threaten or explain; let anyone else see
  her. Afterwards Agneša puts a wet cloth on Katka's head and calls it the
  sun.
- **shape**: one long hot scene. Ends with the tied handful of rye in an
  empty field.

## 05: The Tenth Sheaf (August)
- canon: T5, F10, V4, F2, F4, D2
- **must include**: hail on the Stony a week before the oats are ready; what
  is left is reaped anyway; the tithe counted in the field, every tenth
  sheaf, by Father Hermann himself, who counts fairly and is ashamed to be
  counting; Matej reckons the year aloud for once: rent, tithe, seed, and
  what is left to eat; it does not reach to spring; Father Hermann asks
  Katka, directly and kindly, what the strip is for, and she finds she cannot
  lie to him and does not know enough to tell the truth.
- **must not**: make the priest grasping or the family self-pitying.
- **shape**: the counting of sheaves is the spine. Ends with Jakub looking
  down the valley at the Share, saying nothing.

## 06: Iron (St Michael, September)
- canon: A1, U5, P5, V2, D2, F4, F5 · reveals A1
- **must include**: the settlers' carts arriving for the New Clearing; the
  cord run again along the wood; the autumn ploughing of Under-the-Alders;
  Katka wakes before dawn to the sound of the plough where it should not be;
  Jakub, alone, with both oxen, has the iron share through the Share; Rohan
  has to be beaten to go; the hazel roots tear; it is done by sunrise. His
  reason, said to her without anger: "Now it's ploughed. Now it's ours. Let
  him measure that." The rent is paid at St Michael and Kunrat, shown the
  ploughed strip, takes off his hat and agrees it is not waste.
- **must not**: punish Jakub in this chapter. Nothing happens. The rye is
  sown on it with the rest. That is what is frightening.
- **shape**: sound before sight. Ends with Baba, who has said nothing all day,
  taking the tally stick out, looking at it, and putting it away uncut.

## 07: What Left (October)
- canon: A2, U6, U9, D2, T4, F3
- **must include**: flax pulled and laid to ret; apples picked, and Katka
  leaves the last ones on the tree though nobody tells her to; the
  withdrawals, one at a time and each explicable: the dish by the threshold
  stays full, and sours; Lyska goes dry early; two hives are found empty,
  not dead, simply gone; the well tastes of alder leaves. The rye on the
  ploughed Share comes up with the rest, and thicker.
- **must not**: let anyone name a cause aloud. Agneša says "it's a bad year"
  and it is.
- **shape**: a list, told as a month of work. Ends with Jakub pouring the
  soured milk out and filling the dish himself.

## 08: The Table Laid for the Dead (All Souls and St Martin, November)
- canon: U11, D3, D4, F1, V5, T3 · reveals D3, D4
- **must include**: the pig killed at St Martin; on All Souls' night the
  table left laid, the fire banked, everyone to bed; Katka wakes and Baba is
  sitting at the table in the dark, not alone, though Katka sees only Baba
  (U3); in the morning Baba tells her 1241: the riders, the alders, her
  mother going to the pool and coming back with wet hands, the words of the
  price. "For as long as you would have been dead." Katka asks how long that
  is. Baba: "I never asked. If you ask, they tell you."
- **must not**: describe the Tatars beyond what a nine-year-old saw from
  under roots: horses' legs, a spear's length away.
- **shape**: night, then morning. Ends with Baba's cough, the first time it
  is mentioned.

## 09: Lucy's Stool (St Lucy to Christmas Eve, December)
- canon: H2, T3, T4, F1, F6, V4
- **must include**: threshing with flails; spinning; snow; the customs of
  St Lucy's night told lightly, through Ondro, who wants to make a Lucy
  stool so that he can see who the witches are at midnight mass; Christmas
  Eve supper with straw under the table and a place set for whoever comes;
  Baba is carried to the table; Father Hermann comes up through the snow to
  see her, and they talk a long time with the door shut, and he comes out
  looking like a man who has been given something heavy to carry and has
  agreed to carry it.
- **must not**: tell what was said.
- **shape**: the lightest chapter. Ends with the place set for whoever comes,
  still empty, and the door on the latch.

## 10: News from the Road (January 1301)
- canon: T6, F8, P10, F2, F4
- **must include**: deep cold; wolves heard, not seen; Vavro comes home from
  Štiavnica on foot with a cough, a purse, and the news that the king is
  dead and there is no king; what that means in the village (nothing, and
  then: whose charter is Kunrat's, if the lord changes?); Vavro's silver
  would buy seed and bread to spring; he looks at the ploughed Share under
  snow and asks who did it, and when told, says only, "I'd have done it at
  his age. That's why I left."
- **must not**: resolve the food shortage entirely; let the news of the king
  become plot beyond the charter question.
- **shape**: an arrival. Ends with the two brothers, Matej and Vavro, mending
  harness side by side without talking.

## 11: Carrying Out Death (Lent, February–March 1301)
- canon: A3, A4, D5, U8, U2, U12, V5, F1 · reveals D5, A4
- **must include**: the hungry weeks; Baba gives Katka the tally and tells
  her the last thing: it was never only this house (D5); Baba dies, plainly,
  with the family there; old Pavol comes to the wake and says to Katka, "I
  was in the alders too."; on Death Sunday the girls carry the straw Morena
  to the stream and Katka is the one who puts her in; while the straw is in
  the water Katka, alone at the Black Eye, asks: "What is owed?" The answer,
  from a man with a dripping hem: "A strip." She asks, "That strip?" "A
  strip." She asks nothing more.
- **must not**: have him say one word beyond those; show him to anyone else;
  let Katka ask how long.
- **shape**: death, then the question. This is the chapter in which Katka
  believes. Ends with the straw going out between the two stones.

## 12: The First Notch (St George, April 1301)
- canon: A5, A6, A7, D1, D2, D6, V1, P8, F4 · fixed ending
- **must include**: Jakub chooses the new Share and paces it himself: six by
  two hundred, on the Upper, the best of their land, next to the house; "If
  it's going to cost, it should cost."; Katka goes to Kunrat at the mill and
  shows him where the Lieskovka has cut the bank below the first felled
  alders, and what feeds his race; he looks a long time, and moves his pegs;
  the rye on the old Share is the best in the valley and they will reap it
  and eat it; before dawn on St George Katka takes her own loaf's heel to
  the pool with Ondro, who turns his back without being told; it goes up the
  pool, and down; she cuts one notch on a new hazel rod.
- **must not**: add an epilogue, a moral, or any sign of approval from the
  unseen beyond the bread.
- **shape**: mirrors chapter 01, shorter. Last line is the count: "One."
