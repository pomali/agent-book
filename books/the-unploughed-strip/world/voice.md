# Voice

The two written chapters are the model. When growing a seed, reread one of
them first.

## Narration

- Close third person on **Katka**, past tense. We know what she notices and
  counts, and only sometimes what she thinks. We never enter another head.
- Short and middle-length sentences. Concrete nouns: the thing, its
  material, its weight. Weather and work carry the mood.
- Katka counts. Numbers appear in the prose as she would have them: paces,
  notches, sheaves, hearths.
- Chapters are about 700–1000 words, one or two scenes, and end on an image
  or an action, never on a thought summing things up.

## The unseen

- Described only by what one person's senses could report: a wet hem, bread
  that sinks, milk gone from a dish. No glowing, no music, no voices from
  nowhere.
- The narration never says whether it was real. Katka does not decide until
  chapter 11.
- When one of them speaks, it is brief, literal, and answers exactly the
  question asked (canon U2).
- Never use the words magic, spirit, fairy, demon, creature, supernatural.
  The villagers say *they*, *those*, or nothing.

## Speech

- Plain modern English, no "thee", no dialect spelling, no mock-archaic word
  order. Period comes from what people talk about, not how.
- Baba gives instructions. Matej uses few words. Agneša is dry. Jakub
  reckons in rye. Ondro reports. Kunrat is courteous and exact. Father
  Hermann asks questions and listens to the answers.
- A few native words stay untranslated and unitalicised after first use:
  šoltýs, lán, Baba, Rusadlá, Morena.

## Tone

- Unhurried. The year is long and the book knows it.
- No villains, no fools. Poverty without misery; faith without mockery.
- Hard things happen (a poor harvest, a death in Lent) and are told plainly,
  at their real size.
- Humour comes from Ondro, the miller, and the oxen.
