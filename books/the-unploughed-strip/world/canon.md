# Canon: the world of The Unploughed Strip

Binding. Items marked **[secret → NN]** must not be revealed to the reader, or
known by characters other than those named, before chapter NN.

## P: Place

- P1. The village is **Lieskovany** ("place of hazels"), fourteen hearths, in
  a side valley of the river Hron, in the north of the Kingdom of Hungary.
  It is fictional. The county seat and its castle are a day's walk away and
  are not named.
- P2. The stream is the **Lieskovka**. Where it slows under the alders there
  is a deep pool, the **Black Eye**.
- P3. The **Alder Wood** (*Jelšina*) stands along the stream at the lower edge
  of the village fields.
- P4. The village has three great fields, each divided into strips held by
  the households: **the Upper**, **the Stony**, and **Under-the-Alders**.
- P5. The three-field rotation in this story's year:
  the Upper carries winter rye sown in autumn 1299, reaped July 1300;
  the Stony carries oats and barley sown April 1300, reaped August 1300;
  Under-the-Alders lies fallow through summer 1300, is ploughed, and is sown
  with winter rye at St Michael 1300.
- P6. **The Share**: at the bottom of the family's strips in Under-the-Alders,
  along the wood's edge, a piece six paces wide and two hundred long that the
  family never ploughs and never mows before St John. Hazel and dog-rose have
  come up in it. The village calls it *their share* and does not say whose.
- P7. A wooden chapel of St George stands in the village. The parish church
  is in the larger village down the valley, an hour's walk.
- P8. Up the valley, the šoltýs is founding a new settlement on cleared land,
  the **New Clearing**. He owns the mill below the Alder Wood; its race is fed
  by the Lieskovka.
- P9. Houses are of logs, roofed with thatch or shingle, one heated room with
  an oven and open hearth; smoke leaves through the roof space. Windows are
  small, shuttered, or covered with stretched membrane.
- P10. The silver-mining town of Štiavnica is three days' walk to the south.

## F: The family

They have no surname. The village calls them **those at the Share**.

- F1. **Baba Dobrava**, about 68, widow. Keeps the tally. Small, deaf in one
  ear when it suits her. Speaks in instructions. Wants: to hand over the
  reckoning before she dies. Fears: that she has left it too late.
- F2. **Matej**, 40, her son, head of the household. Careful, tired, fair.
  Wants to owe no one anything. Speaks little; finishes other people's work
  without comment.
- F3. **Agneša**, 36, his wife, from the next valley. Grows and works flax;
  her linen is the family's only source of coin. Practical. Keeps the customs
  because they cost little, and says so.
- F4. **Jakub**, 17, eldest. Strong, quick, impatient. Wants land of his own;
  the New Clearing offers it. Counts everything in sacks of rye.
- F5. **Katarína, called Katka**, 14. The story stays with her. Watchful,
  exact, counts things without meaning to. Does not yet know whether she
  believes.
- F6. **Ondrej, called Ondro**, 9. Herds the geese and, this year for the
  first time, the oxen. Tells truths and inventions in the same voice.
- F7. **Hana**, born at Candlemas (2 February) 1300.
- F8. **Vavro**, 33, Matej's brother. Left for the mines at Štiavnica six
  years ago. Returns in January 1301 with a cough and a purse of silver.
- F9. Animals: oxen **Sivko** (grey, steady) and **Rohan** (wide-horned,
  wilful); the cow **Lyska**; the dog **Bodrík**; geese, hens, two pigs, bees
  in four log hives.
- F10. The family holds a half-*lán* of strips across the three fields. They
  owe rent to the lord in two parts, at St George and at St Michael, and a
  tenth of grain to the Church.

## V: The village

- V1. **Kunrat**, 45, the **šoltýs**: hereditary headman under a charter from
  the lord, come from the German towns three years ago. Holds free land, the
  mill, the right to brew. Judges small disputes. Widower. Direct, hard-
  working, honest in his dealings and blind to what cannot be measured.
- V2. Kunrat's charter grants him the valley's **waste**, land that is not
  ploughed, mown or built on, to settle newcomers, who get twelve rent-free
  years.
- V3. **Gertrúd, called Trudka**, 16, Kunrat's daughter. Speaks both tongues.
  Jakub looks for reasons to be at the mill.
- V4. **Father Hermann**, 28, parish priest since St Michael 1299, a Saxon
  from the Spiš towns. Earnest, learned, kind to the sick, uneasy about what
  his parishioners do at pools and crossroads. He walks up to say mass at the
  chapel on feast days.
- V5. **Old Pavol**, the beekeeper, Baba's age, the only other person alive
  who remembers 1241.
- V6. **Blažej**, the miller, Kunrat's man. Cheerful, a gossip.
- V7. Villagers speak a Slavic tongue; Kunrat's household German; the priest
  Latin at the altar. The story is told in English and does not imitate
  accents.

## T: Time

- T1. The story runs from **St George's Day, 24 April 1300**, to **St George's
  Day, 24 April 1301**.
- T2. Time is told by feast days, sun, and the chapel bell. Nobody knows the
  hour by number or uses the year's number in speech.
- T3. Feasts used: St George 24 Apr; Rusadlá (Pentecost) in May; St John 24
  Jun; St Margaret 13 Jul (harvest begins); St Michael 29 Sep; All Souls
  2 Nov; St Martin 11 Nov; St Lucy 13 Dec; Christmas Eve 24 Dec; Candlemas
  2 Feb; Death Sunday in Lent (the straw Morena is carried out and drowned).
- T4. The farming year: April sow oats and barley, first driving of cattle to
  pasture on St George. May weed, sow flax and millet. June hay, first
  ploughing of the fallow. July reap rye with sickles. August reap oats and
  barley; the tithe is counted in the field. September plough and sow winter
  rye; rent at St Michael. October pull and ret flax, gather mast for pigs,
  pick apples. November slaughter, break and comb flax. Winter: thresh with
  flails, spin with distaff and spindle, mend. Late winter is the hungry
  time. Lent: lambing and the first ploughing.
- T5. 1300 is a poor year in the valley: a dry May, hail on the Stony in
  August.
- T6. Outside events that may reach the village as rumour: the Pope has
  declared a year of pilgrimage to Rome (1300). King Andrew, last of the
  house of Árpád, dies in January 1301; news arrives within the month, and
  nobody knows who will be king.

## U: The unseen

What they are is never explained. The story shows only what a person could
notice.

- U1. **Exchange only.** They give nothing freely and take nothing unpaid. A
  thing offered and accepted is a bargain.
- U2. **They do not lie.** They answer only what was asked, and exactly.
- U3. **One witness.** Nothing unseen ever shows itself to more than one
  person at a time. Whatever happens can afterwards be told another way.
- U4. **No spells.** People have no magic. They have customs: bread, salt,
  milk, first and last things, thresholds, names not spoken.
- U5. **Iron hurts them.** Cut iron in their ground is an injury as well as a
  theft.
- U6. **They do not strike. They withdraw.** Milk, water, bees, luck, sleep.
- U7. **The Church is neither their enemy nor their master.** Blessings do not
  drive them off; they do not mock the mass. They are older than the
  question.
- U8. **Him Below the Alders.** In the Black Eye. When seen: a man of no
  particular age in a coat whose left hem drips. Never named. Receives the
  St George bread. Keeps accounts.
- U9. **The Householder.** A pale snake that lives under the threshold. Gets
  the first milk in a dish. While it stays, the house does well. It must
  never be harmed or shut out.
- U10. **The Noon Wife.** In the grain at midday during harvest, when sensible
  people rest. Tall, white, the air shaking round her. She asks questions.
  Whoever is found working through noon must answer until the bell, and
  truthfully.
- U11. **The Dead.** On All Souls' night the table is left laid. They come for
  what was theirs. They are family and are treated so.
- U12. **Morena.** The straw figure carried out and drowned on Death Sunday is
  only straw, except for as long as it is in the water.

## D: The debt

- D1. Baba keeps a **hazel tally stick** with one notch for each year paid.
  On St George 1300 she cuts the fifty-ninth.
- D2. The yearly payments: on St George, a heel of bread into the Black Eye;
  the first milk of the year to the Householder; at harvest, the last
  handful of rye left standing and tied; in autumn, the last apples left on
  the tree; and always, the Share unploughed.
- D3. **[secret → 08]** In the spring of 1241 the Tatars rode through the
  valley. Dobrava, nine years old, hid in the Alder Wood with her mother and
  two brothers. Her mother went to the pool alone and came back. The riders
  passed within a spear's length and did not see them. Most of the village
  died or was taken.
- D4. **[secret → 08]** The price her mother agreed was "a strip, and the
  reckoning kept, for as long as you would have been dead." Nobody knows how
  long that is. Dobrava has never asked, because an asked question is
  answered (U2).
- D5. **[secret → 11]** The debt is not for the family's lives alone but for
  the village's: the fourteen hearths descend from the few who were in the
  alders that day. Old Pavol was one. He knows; he has never spoken of it.
- D6. The tally passes to whoever is shown it. Baba shows it to Katka, not to
  Jakub, on St George 1300.

## A: The arc (fixed points)

- A1. **[secret → 06]** At the autumn ploughing, Jakub ploughs the Share with
  the iron share, alone, at dawn. His reason is good: under Kunrat's charter
  unploughed land is waste, and the cord has been run along the wood's edge.
  He ploughs it to keep it.
- A2. After that the withdrawals come one at a time (U6): the Householder is
  gone from the threshold; Lyska's milk; the bees; the well tastes of alder.
  Nothing that could not also be a bad year.
- A3. Baba Dobrava dies in Lent 1301, having told Katka everything and given
  her the tally.
- A4. Katka goes to the Black Eye alone and asks the question nobody asked:
  what is owed. The answer is: **a strip**. Not that strip.
- A5. The family gives a new Share. Jakub chooses it, and chooses good land.
- A6. Kunrat's clearing stops short of the Alder Wood, for his own reason:
  Katka shows him that the alders hold the bank that feeds his mill race.
- A7. On St George 1301 Katka cuts the first notch on a new stick.

## H: History and anachronism

Use for *With notes*, and check before inventing.

- H1. **Attested for the period**: three-field rotation; heavy plough drawn
  by oxen; sickle for grain, scythe for hay; flail; water mills; distaff and
  spindle; rents at St George and St Michael; the Church tithe; settlement of
  villages under German law by a *šoltýs* (scultetus) with rent-free years for
  settlers; silver mining at Štiavnica; the Mongol invasion of Hungary in
  1241–42; the Jubilee of 1300; the death of Andrew III on 14 January 1301
  and the succession struggle after it; tally sticks for reckoning.
- H2. **Folk belief recorded later, used here as if old**: the house snake;
  the water man; the noon woman; laying the table on All Souls; the customs
  of St Lucy's night; carrying out Morena; first driving of cattle on St
  George. Their forms are known from much later collections; their age is
  uncertain.
- H3. **Invented**: Lieskovany and everyone in it; the Share; the debt.
- H4. **Not in this world**: potatoes, maize, tomatoes, peppers, beans of the
  New World, tobacco, sunflowers, buckwheat, sugar, coffee, tea; chimneys,
  glass windows, clocks, forks, spinning wheels, paper, printed anything,
  gunpowder; surnames; anyone reading except priests and the šoltýs's clerk;
  sheep-cheese shepherding of the later Wallachian kind; the words "medieval",
  "peasant" as an insult, "Slovakia", "magic", "spirit", "fairy".
- H5. Food: rye bread, oat and millet porridge, peas, lentils, cabbage,
  turnips, onions, garlic, curds, eggs, apples, plums, nuts, honey,
  mushrooms; pork in winter; fish on fast days; small beer, sometimes mead.
- H6. Dress: linen and hempen shirts, woollen outer clothes, leather shoes
  tied with thongs or bare feet. Married women cover their hair.
- H7. Coin is scarce: small silver pennies. Most dealings are in kind.
- H8. The calendar is Julian; seasons run about a week later against the date
  than a modern reader expects.
