# Source

- **Work**: Sophocles, *Ajax*, written about 442 BCE (date uncertain).
- **Translation**: Francis Storr, 1913.
- **Edition used**: Standard Ebooks,
  <https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/sophocles/ajax/francis-storr>, revision
  `e6b9504`, transcribed and produced by Emma Sweeney from scans at the
  Internet Archive.
- **Rights**: the translation is believed to be in the public domain in the
  United States; Standard Ebooks dedicates its own editorial work to the
  public domain under CC0 1.0. Copyright terms differ between countries. A
  reader outside the United States should check their own.

## What was done to the text

The play was converted from the edition's XHTML to Markdown and divided into
ten scene files. Nothing was cut, reordered or reworded.

- Verse lines are kept as lines (two trailing spaces mark each break).
- Indented lyric lines are indented with em spaces.
- Sung passages are marked *(sung)* after the speaker's name, following the
  edition's markup.
- Stage directions are in italics in parentheses. They are Storr's.
- Storr's seven footnotes are kept, each at the end of the scene it belongs
  to. His prefatory "Argument" is not reproduced; its content is in
  `canon/facts.md`.
- Each speech has been given a number, `scene.speech`. Scene divisions,
  scene titles and speech numbers are this edition's additions.

## Corrections

Two evident slips in the source were corrected:

- scene 09, the third stasimon: "F'en the slumbers" → "E'en the slumbers".
- the list of characters (used in canon, not reproduced) reads "Treucer" for
  Teucer, and names Ajax's mother "Euboea" where the play's text has
  "Eriboea". Canon follows the text.

One oddity is left as found: the stage direction at the end of scene 06,
"Exeunt Ajax alone on the sea-shore…", runs two directions together (the
Chorus and Tecmessa leave; the scene changes; Ajax is discovered alone).
